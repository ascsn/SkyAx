\subsubsection{Detailed Description}
This module contains routines and variables to solve the Coulomb problem. 

The Coulomb field is determined from the charge density by the Poisson equation \[\Delta\Phi(\mathbf{r}) = 4\pi e^2\rho(\mathbf{r}) \quad.\] Although the given density $ \rho(\mathbf{r}) $ is well localized, the resulting Coulomb field $\Phi(\mathbf{r})$ is long ranged. The behavior at large distance is determined by the multipole moments \[ q_{lm}=\int d^3r\,r^lY_{lm}\rho(\mathbf{r})\quad.\] We deal with axially symmetric systems which have only non-\/zero moments for $m=0$ and ignore $m\neq 0$ in the following.

To cope with the long range behavior, we separate the problem into a longe-\/range part and a short-\/range part. \[\rho=\rho^\mathrm{(long)}+\rho^\mathrm{(short)}\quad,\] \[ \rho^\mathrm{(long)}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l=0}^4\sum_{m=-l}^lq_{lm} \rho_l(r)Y_{lm}(\Omega_\mathbf{r})\quad,\] where the long-\/range reference densities are defined as \[\rho_l(r)=\mathcal{N}_l\frac{r^{l+2}}{R_\mathrm{max}+r^l} e^{-r^2/\mu_l^2}\quad,\] \[\mathcal{N}_l: 1=4\pi\int_0^\infty dr\,r^{l+2}\,\rho_l(r)\quad.\] The choice of the width parameter is $\mu_l=R_\mathrm{min}/(6\log2)$ The reference radii are $R_\mathrm{min}$ the smaller of the extensions in $z$ and $r$ direction and $R_\mathrm{max}$ the larger one.

The Coulomb problem for the reference densities becomes a simple radial Poisson equation which can be solved in inexpensive manner by a couple of radial integrals. The solution for the long-\/range part is thus composed as \[\Phi=\Phi^\mathrm{(long)}+\Phi^\mathrm{(short)}\quad,\] \[\Phi^\mathrm{(long)}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l=0}^4q_{l0} \Phi_l(r)Y_{l0}(\Omega_\mathbf{r})\quad,\] \[\Phi_l(r)=\frac{e^2}{r^{l+1}}\int_0^r dr'\,{r'}^l\,\rho_l(r')+ e^2{r}^l\int_r^\infty dr'\,\frac{1}{{r'}^{l-1}}\,\rho_l(r')\quad.\]

There remains the Poisson equation for the short-\/range part which is simplified by the boundary conditions $\Phi^\mathrm{(short)} \big|_\mathrm{bounds}=0$, i.\+e. that the field vanishes everywhere at the bounds of the box. We solve it by damped gradient iteration similarly as for the s.\+p. wavefunctions.
