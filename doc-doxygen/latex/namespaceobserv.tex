Computes the total energy directly from densities. 

The different contribution to the total energy are
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item $ T $\+: the total kinetic energy calculated as \[ T=\sum_q \frac{\hbar^2}{2m_q}\int\D^3 r\, \tau_q \] with $ \tau_q $ the isospin-\/specific kinetic density.
\item $ E_0 $\+: The $ b_0 $ and $ b'_0 $-\/dependent part is \[ E_0=\int \D^3\!r\,\left(\frac{b_0}{2}\rho^2-\frac{b_0'}{2}\sum_q\rho_q^2\right). \]
\item $ E_{1}$\+: kinetic terms containing the coefficients $ b_1 $ and $ b_1' $\+: \[ E_1=\int \D^3\!r\,\left(b_1[\rho\tau-{\vec\jmath\,}{}^2] -b_1'\sum_q[\rho_q\tau_q-{\vec\jmath_q}{}^2]\right).\]
\item $ E_{2} $\+: terms containing the coefficients $ b_2 $ and $ b_2' $. They involve the Laplacians of the densities. \[ E_2=\int \D^3\!r\,\left(-\frac{b_2}{2}\rho\Delta\rho +\frac{b_2'}{2}\sum_q\rho_q\Delta\rho_q\right). \]
\item $ E_{3} $\+: The many-\/body contribution is given by \[ E_{3}=\int \D^3\!r\,\left(\frac{b_3}{3}\rho^{\alpha+2} -\frac{b_3'}{3}\rho^\alpha\sum_q\rho_q^2\right). \]
\item $ E_\mathrm{ls} $\+: the spin-\/orbit energy \[ E_\mathrm{ls}=\int \D^3\!r\,\biggl( -b_4[\rho\nabla\cdot\vec J+ \vec s\cdot(\nabla\times\vec\jmath)]-b_4'\sum_q[\rho_q\nabla\cdot\vec J_q+ \vec s_q\cdot(\nabla\times\vec\jmath_q)]\biggr) \]
\item $ E_\mathrm{C} $\+: the Coulomb energy. It consists of the standard expression for a charge distribution in its own field (Hartree term) plus the exchange term in the Slater approximation. The formula is \[ E_\mathrm{C} = \frac{e^2}{2}\int \D^3\!r \D^3\!r' \frac{\rho_p(\vec{r})\rho_p(\vec{r'})}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r'}|} -\int \D^3\!r\,\frac{3e^2}{4}\left(\frac3{\pi}\right)^{\tfrac1{3}}\rho_p^{4/3} \] where $ e $ is the elementary charge with $ e^2=1.43989 $ MeV $ \cdot $fm. 
\end{DoxyItemize}\index{observ@{observ}!energ2@{energ2}}

The fourier-\/bessel interpolation runs into problems with round-\/off errors if {\ttfamily qeff} comes too close to a multiple of $ \pi $. We switch to an explicit treatment of the pole-\/term in that case. {\ttfamily precis} defines the neighbourhood of a pole-\/term for which the routine switches to an explicit laurent expansion.

The pairing energy is evaluated as follows\+:

$ E_\mathrm{pair} $\+: the pairing energy. It consists of a contact pairing interaction involving the pairing densities $ \xi_q $ augmented by an optional density dependence. The formula is \[ E_{\rm pair} = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{q\in\{p,n\}}V_\mathrm{pair,q} \int \D^3r |\xi_q|^2 \left[1 -\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0,\mathrm{pair}}}\right]\;. \] It contains a continuous switch, the parameter $ \rho_{0,\mathrm{pair}} $. A pure $ \delta $-\/interaction (DI), also called volume pairing, is recovered for $ \rho_{0,\mathrm{pair}}\longrightarrow\infty $. The general case is the density dependent $ \delta $-\/interaction (D\+D\+DI). A typical value near matter equilibrium density $ \rho_{0,\mathrm{pair}}=0.16 $ fm $ ^{-3} $ concentrates pairing to the surface. The most flexible choice is to consider $ \rho_{0,\mathrm{pair}} $ as an additional free parameter. Actual adjustments with this option deliver a form of the pairing functional which stays in between the extremes of volume and surface pairing.
